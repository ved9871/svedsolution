If you sell loss prevention technology, such as fog cannons, EAS systems, CCTV analytics or security fog, you already know the problem with standard SEO advice. It assumes that lots of people are searching. In your category, they are not. A term like `security fog system` gets roughly 320 searches a month in the US and 170 in the UK. `fog cannon` sits around 320 in the US. These numbers are real. They describe a market where a single installed contract can be worth tens of thousands.

That gap between tiny search numbers and large sales is why most loss prevention companies either overspend on the wrong keywords or give up on search completely. Both are mistakes. Here is the method that works for this niche in the US and UK, and how to become the brand that gets recommended when a specifier (the person who chooses the equipment) or an AI assistant answers a buying question.

## Why SEO for loss prevention companies is a different game

Three facts change everything:

1. **Search numbers are small, so chasing the biggest terms is a trap.** Ranking first for a term with 320 searches is worth far less than being the trusted source across fifty specification and application searches that lead to sales.
2. **The buyer is not the end user.** Specifiers, installers, facilities managers and procurement teams research very differently from a shopper. They want standards, spec sheets and evidence. Marketing adjectives do not help them.
3. **Buying takes a long time and involves several people.** Your content has to survive being forwarded, compared and re-read by three people over three months.

The businesses that win use search to *create* demand and supply *evidence*, rather than only catching demand that already exists. That is the thinking behind our [security and loss prevention SEO](/security-loss-prevention-seo/) work. It is the opposite of a general keyword campaign.

Two things stand out. First, the broad terms (`loss prevention`, `retail loss prevention`) get a lot of searches but are general. Their results are full of dictionary-style pages and directories, and the searches rarely lead to a purchase. Second, the money is in the **long tail of specification and application searches**: specific, low-volume searches such as `security fog system cost`, `fog cannon security` and `security smoke system`. They are easy to rank for (KD 4–12) and come from people who are close to buying. That is where a focused site wins.

## The five-part method

### 1. Category-education content that creates demand
Most buyers do not wake up searching for your product name. They search for the *problem*: smash-and-grab losses, ATM attacks, stockroom shrinkage. Publish the guides that answer those problems and introduce the type of solution: how security fog works, response times, coverage per cannon, what it means for insurance. You are teaching the market, and demand follows the teacher. Pair this with a planned [content strategy](/services/content-strategy/) so each guide leads to a page that sells.

### 2. Specification-grade product pages
A specifier looking into `security fog system cost` or discharge rate does not want a big banner image. They want numbers: coverage volume, activation time, refill cycle, certifications, power draw. Build pages that read like a datasheet with a plain explanation around the numbers. These are the pages procurement teams forward. They are also the pages AI tools quote, because they are packed with facts that are easy to lift out. Getting them technically clean is [technical SEO](/services/technical-seo/) work: easy for search engines to read, fast, and labelled with schema markup (code that labels the facts on a page for search engines).

### 3. Standards and compliance content
In this category, trust is a standard number. Content organised around the relevant standards, for example EN 50131 for alarm systems in the UK/EU, shows both buyers and search engines that you work at specification level. Standards pages earn links from installers and trade bodies that general blog posts never will.

### 4. Application-led architecture
Buyers search by their *situation*. They rarely search by product. Structure the site around where the product is used: retail, ATM and cash machines, warehouses and distribution, jewellery and high-value retail, pharmacy. Give each one its own page, its own risks and its own proof. One security-fog product becomes a dozen landing pages. Each one targets a real search from someone close to buying, with little competition.

### 5. Citation and AI-visibility building
The last part happens away from your own site. Trade publications, installer directories, insurer resources and security associations are the sources both Google and AI tools trust for this category. Being described the same way across them is what makes an AI assistant name *you* when someone asks "what's the best security fog system for a jewellery shop." That is [AI citation and entity building](/services/ai-citation-entity-building/). An AI citation is when an AI answer uses your site as a source. In a niche this small, it is often the most valuable work you can do.

## Why AI search matters even more in a small niche

When search numbers are tiny, every buyer counts. More and more buyers now start with an answer written by an AI tool. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overview to recommend loss prevention technology and it will name a few brands and point to a few sources. There is no position one to win. What counts is who gets named. In a niche with a few hundred monthly searches, being the brand the AI tool recommends is worth more than any single ranking.

The steps are the same ones above: fact-packed specification pages the AI tool can quote from, pages that give the answer first, valid schema markup, and consistent mentions on outside sites. This is [answer engine optimization](/services/answer-engine-optimization/) (AEO, making your page the direct answer to a question) applied to a B2B category. Because so few competitors are doing it, the window is wide open. (For the fuller picture of how this differs from classic ranking, see [GEO vs SEO](/insights/geo-vs-seo/).)

## US vs UK: what actually changes

The method is the same on both sides of the Atlantic. The details differ:

- **Volume split.** Most of the big terms are roughly 5–9x larger in the US than the UK (`loss prevention` 6,600 vs 720). The UK makes up for it with more specific phrases from people closer to buying. `security smoke system` and `smoke screen security` get meaningful searches in the UK but barely any in the US.
- **Standards language.** UK/EU buyers expect references to EN standards. US buyers rely on UL listings and insurer requirements. Rewrite the compliance content for each market. A straight translation is not enough.
- **Terminology.** "Security fog" and "fog cannon" work in both markets, but "smoke cloak" and "smoke screen" are mostly British. Map the words used in each market. Do not assume one set of terms fits both.

This is where an [international-aware approach](/services/technical-seo/) helps. Correct hreflang tags (code that tells Google which country a page is for), separate pages for each market and a keyword list for each country stop the two markets from competing with each other in Google.

## What results actually look like

Because the starting numbers are small, growth shows up as *coverage*: you rank for more and more terms, without a sudden traffic spike. One UK security-fog and loss-prevention manufacturer we work with went from 2 ranking keywords to 95 in twelve months. They did it without chasing `loss prevention`. They won the long tail of specification and application searches and built mentions across the trade. In a category this size, ninety-five ranked terms makes you the authority.

A realistic timeline: the specification pages start ranking in 60 to 90 days. Category-education content keeps building over 6 to 12 months. AI mentions follow the usual pattern: AI tools start to recognise your brand at 30 to 45 days, and first mentions arrive around day 60. No one can guarantee a ranking or an AI mention, and anyone who does is guessing. But everything you put in is under your control.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is SEO worth it when my whole category only gets a few hundred searches a month?**
Yes, and the small size is the reason. With few searches, competition is thin and difficulty is low (KD 4–12 across the security-fog group of terms), so a focused site can lead the category cheaply. And because each sale is large, a handful of buyers a month at the specification stage can pay for the whole programme.

**Should I target broad terms like "loss prevention" or "retail loss prevention"?**
Cover them for awareness, but do not build your plan on them. They get a lot of searches but are general, their results are full of dictionary and directory pages, and they rarely lead to a purchase. The revenue is in specification and application searches like `security fog system cost` and `fog cannon security`.

**How is SEO for loss prevention companies different from normal SEO?**
The technical foundation is identical. What changes is the approach: creating demand before catching it, specification pages in place of marketing copy, a site structured by application, standards content and trade mentions. That is because your buyer is a specifier or installer, who does not research like a shopper.

**How do I get recommended by ChatGPT or Google's AI Overview in this niche?**
Publish fact-packed specification content that is easy to quote. Add FAQ and Organization schema. Most importantly, make sure your brand is described the same way across the trade sources AI tools already trust. In a small category, that agreement between outside sites is often the deciding factor.

**Does this work for both the US and UK?**
Yes, if you adapt it to each market. Keep the method. Change the standards references (EN vs UL) and the terminology (smoke screen vs security fog). Map keywords for each market so the two sites support each other and do not compete.

## The bottom line

Loss prevention is a niche with few searches and large sales. That is an advantage once you stop using SEO built for big search numbers. Create demand with category education. Prove it with specification pages and standards. Structure the site by application. Build the trade mentions that make both Google and AI tools recommend you. Do that steadily and a few hundred monthly searches becomes a steady flow of enquiries.
