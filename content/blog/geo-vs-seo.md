Three acronyms are being sold as three products: SEO, GEO and AEO. In practice they describe one underlying change and three places where you can act on it. Here is how we think about it in our own work.

## The one difference that matters

Traditional search returns a **list**. Generative search, where AI writes the reply, returns an **answer**.

That single change alters what you are competing for:

| | SEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|
| **Competing for** | A position in a ranked list | Being named inside an AI-written answer |
| **What competes** | The page | The brand |
| **Main driver** | Relevant pages + backlinks | A clear brand identity + agreement between outside sites |
| **How you win** | Outrank a specific competing page | Be remembered more often than competing brands |
| **Time to see results** | Days to weeks | 60 to 90 days |
| **How well it holds** | Moderate — a better page can displace you | High — trust in a brand builds slowly and lasts |

In a ranked list, a new page can push out an old one on the strength of that page alone. In an AI-written answer, page-against-page comparison never happens. The AI tool recalls which brands it connects with the question, then finds sources to back that up.

**That is why GEO cannot be won with on-page work.** On-page work means changes to your own pages. The AI tool was never unsure about your page. It was unsure about your brand.

## Where AEO fits

[AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)](/services/answer-engine-optimization/) is the narrowest of the three and the most concrete. It is about owning the **direct answer to a specific question**: the featured snippet (the answer box at the top of Google), the People Also Ask box, the summary paragraph at the top of an AI Overview.

- **AEO works at page level and is about structure.** Headings written as questions, FAQ schema markup (code that labels your questions and answers), a direct answer in the first 100 words.
- **GEO works at brand level and is about reputation.** AI tools knowing exactly who you are, mentions on other sites, the same description everywhere.

You can do excellent AEO and still never get named by ChatGPT. AEO makes your page easy to quote. GEO makes your brand easy to remember. You need both.

## The overlap is bigger than the difference

Here is the part that gets lost in the hype. Most GEO signals are classic SEO signals, used by a different system for finding content:

- **Crawlability.** A page a bot cannot open cannot be added to a search index or used in an answer.
- **Rendering.** Content that only exists after JavaScript runs in the browser is invisible to most AI crawlers.
- **Structure.** A clean order of headings makes it easier to pull out a passage and to split a page into pieces.
- **Schema.** Structured data feeds both rich results in Google and an AI tool's understanding of who you are.
- **Freshness.** Newer content counts for more in both systems.
- **Authority.** Links and mentions matter to both, in different proportions.

Roughly 70% of the work is shared. **Anyone selling you GEO without touching your technical health is selling you a report.**

## Where they genuinely diverge

The remaining 30% is where GEO is its own kind of work:

1. **What other sites say outweighs polish on your own pages.** An AI tool that sees your brand described identically across G2, Crunchbase, Reddit and three industry blogs trusts that pattern more than anything on your own domain. Classic SEO barely touches this outside of link building. And link building chases the wrong thing: a link strength score (domain rating), when what counts is a consistent description.

2. **There is no position to measure.** No "#1". You measure how often you are mentioned, your share of mentions against competitors, and which specific sources get used. That takes [controlled prompt testing and AI visibility monitoring](/services/ai-visibility-monitoring/) on a fixed routine. A rank tracker cannot do it.

3. **A consistent brand identity is a hard requirement.** If your brand name is written three different ways across your site and your social profiles, you have split your own identity into pieces. This costs nothing to fix, and almost every site we review fails it.

4. **The preferred format is different.** Ranked search tolerates long essay content. AI tools strongly prefer dense content: tables, side-by-side comparisons, step-by-step frameworks, FAQs with real paragraph answers. Story openings work against you.

## So which do you invest in?

Wrong question. The right order:

1. **Fix [technical foundations](/services/technical-seo/) first.** Both SEO and GEO depend on them. This step cannot be skipped.
2. **Run AEO on your money pages.** These are the pages closest to a sale. It is the cheapest option, gives the fastest feedback and improves classic rankings too.
3. **Run [GEO](/generative-engine-optimization/) continuously in the background.** It is the slowest to show results and the hardest for rivals to undo once it is in place. The window is currently open in most categories.

If your budget only covers one, and your technical health is sound, start GEO now. Traditional rankings can be improved at any time. Trust in your brand takes 90 days minimum to build and roughly a year to become hard to dislodge. It also gets more expensive every quarter you wait, because in time you stop building a position and start trying to take one from someone else.

## The honest caveat

Nobody controls these models and there is no way to submit your site. What you control is every input that makes a mention likely: a clear brand identity, agreement between outside sites, content that is easy to find and quote, and freshness. Anyone guaranteeing you a ChatGPT mention is guessing, and anyone promising it inside two weeks has not measured it. If you want the practical method, see [how to rank in ChatGPT](/insights/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is GEO the same as SEO?**
No. SEO aims for a position in a ranked list of links, driven mainly by relevant pages and backlinks. GEO aims to make you the brand an AI tool names inside its answer, driven mainly by a clear brand identity and agreement between outside sites. They share a technical foundation but differ sharply after it, which is why a site that crawlers cannot read fails at both.

**Does GEO replace SEO?**
No. They run together. Traditional ranked search still brings in a large share of revenue, and AI answers increasingly shape the decision before the click. Working on one and ignoring the other means missing buyers. The right move is to do things in order: technical foundation first, AEO on money pages, GEO continuously in the background.

**Is GEO the same as AEO?**
No. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) works at page level and is about structure. It makes a specific page the quotable answer to a specific question, using question headings and FAQ schema. GEO works at brand level and is about reputation. It gets an AI tool to remember your brand in the first place. AEO shows results faster. GEO is harder to undo once in place. Most brands need both.

**How long does GEO take to work?**
On a typical timeline: AI tools start to recognise your brand at 30 to 45 days, first mentions come around day 60, consistent mentions by day 90, and stable visibility in your category at 9 to 12 months. It is slower than classic ranking work and lasts longer once it holds, which is why the cost of waiting grows.

**Can I do GEO myself?**
Parts of it, yes. Schema markup, answer-first structure and freshness can be done in-house once someone sets the standard. The harder pieces need outside relationships and careful measurement: building mentions on other sites, studying where competitors are mentioned, and running controlled prompt tests every week.
