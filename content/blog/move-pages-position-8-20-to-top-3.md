This works **only** for pages already ranking between positions 8 and 20. Do not run it on new or low-trust pages. There is nothing to build on.

## Before you start

Confirm all three before starting:

- The page is indexed (it appears in Google's results)
- It ranks between 8 and 20 for at least one keyword that matters
- It has both impressions and clicks in Search Console

If any of these fail, stop. This process will not work and you need a different approach.

## Step 1: Find the pages

In Google Search Console: **Performance → Search results → Pages** tab. Set the date range to the last 28 to 90 days, turn on **Average position**, and sort for pages sitting between 8 and 20.

These pages have Google's trust, some relevance, and weak optimisation. That mix is exactly what you want.

## Step 2: Get the queries for each page

This is the step people skip, and it is the one that matters. For each target page:

1. Click the page URL
2. Switch to the **Queries** tab
3. Export query, impressions, clicks and position
4. Download as CSV

That CSV tells you what is wrong. Query data for the whole site will not work. You need the queries for that one page.

## Step 3: Find the search intent gap

Upload the CSV to an AI chat tool (an LLM) and ask it to find:

- Queries with high impressions but few clicks
- Queries ranking between positions 8 and 20
- Queries that suggest a different or stronger intent (what the searcher wants) than the page serves now

Then have it group the queries by intent (informational, commercial, transactional), spot keywords the page does not clearly cover, flag which deserve their own subheadings, and propose exact H2 and H3 placements.

Nine times out of ten the finding is the same: **the page half-answers a question the searcher is actually asking**.

## Step 4: Rewrite the title and meta description for clicks, not rankings

Write ten title options of 60 characters at most and five meta descriptions of 150 to 155 characters. Titles must match the main intent you found in step 3. Use attention-grabbing words only where they truly fit, and avoid clickbait.

**Pick one title and one meta description. Do not test several at once.** You will not be able to tell which change worked.

## Step 5: Match the content to the searches

Work the missing queries naturally into the H2 headings, the first 100 words, and image alt text where relevant. Expand thin sections to make them clearer. Extra length is not the aim.

Do not stuff in keywords, do not pad the page with AI filler, and do not rewrite the whole page unless it is aimed at the wrong intent entirely. **The goal is a closer match. A longer page does not help.**

## Step 6: Internal linking (where the real gains are)

This is where most of the movement comes from. Find pages on your site that cover related topics, already get traffic, and can link to the target page naturally within their text.

Rules we follow:

- Roughly one internal link per 50 words
- Links must sit inside the body text and fit the sentence. Footer and sidebar links do not count
- Use link text that partly matches the keyword. Do not use the exact keyword every time
- Add one or two links out to respected outside sources if there are none, roughly one per 150 words

## Step 7: Quick technical check

Five minutes: confirm Core Web Vitals (Google's page speed tests) are not failing, no canonical tags conflict (they tell Google which version of a page is the main one), the page is not orphaned (other pages link to it), and it reads cleanly on mobile. **Fix only what is broken.** Do not over-optimise.

## Step 8: Request indexing and wait

Request indexing through URL Inspection, then do nothing for 14 to 21 days. Track average position, CTR (the share of people who click) and impressions.

CTR improves first. Rankings follow.

## What to expect

Done correctly, pages move from 8-15 into the top 3, CTR improves before position does, and no backlinks are required.

If nothing moves, there are only two likely explanations. Either the intent mismatch runs deeper than these page changes could fix, or competitors cover the whole topic with more authority and you need a group of linked pages rather than a single page.

## Where it works and where it fails

**Works on:** blogs, landing pages, SaaS pages, service pages, affiliate content.

**Fails on:** brand-new sites, pages below position 30, and queries where Google answers on the results page itself, so almost nobody clicks.
