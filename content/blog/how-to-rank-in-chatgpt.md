"How do I rank in ChatGPT?" is the right instinct and the wrong word. There is no rank. A chat answer is written fresh each time, so there is no list and no position one to win. What you are really competing for is whether the AI tool **names your brand** and **cites your page** (uses it as a source) when it answers a question your buyer asks. This guide is the method we use to make that happen.

## First, understand what you are optimising

When ChatGPT answers, two things decide whether you appear:

1. **Recall.** Does the AI tool already connect your brand with the topic? This comes from repeated, consistent mentions on sites it trusts. It is about your reputation, more than any one page.
2. **Retrieval.** When ChatGPT browses the web or uses a connected search index, can it open your page, pull out a clean passage and credit it to you? This comes down to how your site is built and how your pages are laid out.

You need both. A brand with strong recall but a site AI tools cannot read gets described from memory, without a link. A perfectly structured page from a brand with no recall gets passed over for a competitor the AI tool already trusts.

| | Google ranking | ChatGPT mention |
|---|---|---|
| **What you win** | A position in a list | Being named inside the answer |
| **What competes** | The page | The brand |
| **Main driver** | Relevance + backlinks | A clear brand identity + agreement between outside sites |
| **How you measure** | Rank tracker | How often you are mentioned, your share of mentions |
| **Time to see results** | Days to weeks | 60 to 90 days |

## The method: five steps

### 1. Make your pages easy for ChatGPT to find and read

If ChatGPT, its crawler (the program that fetches pages for it) or a connected search index cannot open and read your page, nothing else matters. In practice:

- **Render your content on the server.** Anything that only appears after JavaScript runs in the visitor's browser often looks like an empty shell to an AI crawler. If your key content is not in the raw HTML, fix that first.
- **Do not block the crawlers you want mentions from.** Check `robots.txt` (the file that tells crawlers what they may read) and your rules for AI crawlers (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended). If you block them, your pages will never be read.
- **Publish an `llms.txt`** at your domain root. This is a short file that describes your site for AI tools. Few sites use it yet and it is not a ranking factor, but it removes doubt and costs almost nothing.

This is the [LLM SEO](/services/llm-seo/) layer: the technical base of being visible to AI tools. LLM means large language model, the technology behind ChatGPT.

### 2. Answer the question in the first 100 words

Language models look for **answers**. They skip long essays. Every page should open with a direct answer of 40 to 60 words to the exact question it targets, before any background or story. Then back it up with a layout the AI tool can split into clean pieces:

- H2 subheadings written as questions
- Short paragraphs, one idea each
- Tables, side-by-side comparisons and numbered steps
- Real data and specific numbers, with a visible last-updated date

What to stop writing: long story openings, copy stuffed with keywords, and opinion without evidence. This answer-first habit is [Answer Engine Optimization](/services/answer-engine-optimization/) (AEO). It also wins featured snippets (the answer boxes at the top of Google).

### 3. Add the schema markup that matches how AI models read

Schema markup is code that labels what is on your page. It helps AI tools work out who you are and pull out the right passage. The most useful types:

- **FAQPage / QAPage.** Question-and-answer markup is the format AI answer tools quote most often.
- **Article.** Author, dates, headline.
- **Organization + sameAs.** Shows that your brand is the same business everywhere it appears on the web.

### 4. Build consistent mentions on outside sites

This is the strongest driver of whether ChatGPT names you, and it happens **off your own domain**. When the AI tool sees your brand described the same way across the platforms it already trusts for your category, it treats that repetition as agreement.

- Settle on one official brand name and description, written identically everywhere.
- Get listed on the outside sites AI tools trust in **your industry**: review databases for SaaS, credentialed directories for clinics, marketplaces and trust platforms for retailers, trade press for manufacturers.
- Study where competitors are mentioned: find the brands already named in AI answers for your searches, list every site quoted alongside them, and earn a place in the same sources.

This is [AI citation and entity building](/services/ai-citation-entity-building/), and it is why work on your own pages alone stops paying off after a point.

### 5. Keep it fresh and measure it

Newer content counts for more, so update your most important pages on a regular routine and show the date of the change. Then measure. A channel you cannot measure is one you are guessing at:

- Run a fixed set of prompts (test questions) every week across ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini.
- Track how often your brand is named, which of your pages get used, and your share of mentions against named competitors.

That measuring routine is [AI visibility monitoring](/services/ai-visibility-monitoring/).

## How long it takes

The pattern is consistent, and it is slower than classic ranking work:

- **Day 30 to 45.** AI tools start to recognise your brand.
- **Day 60.** First mentions typically appear.
- **Day 90.** Consistent mentions across related prompts.
- **Month 9 to 12.** Your lead in the category becomes hard for others to take.

The cost of waiting grows. Early on, you are building a place in what the AI tool connects with your topic. Later, you are trying to push out a brand it already trusts, which gets harder and more expensive every quarter.

## The honest caveat

No one controls these models. There is no submission form, approved list or paid placement for unpaid mentions. Anyone guaranteeing you a ChatGPT mention is guessing, and anyone promising it in two weeks has not measured it. What you control is every input that makes a mention likely: pages that can be read, answer-first structure, schema markup, agreement between outside sites, and freshness. Do those consistently and the mentions follow.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you actually rank number one in ChatGPT?**
No. An AI answer has no numbered positions. You compete to be named in the answer and used as a source. The closest thing to "ranking" is your share of mentions: how often you are named compared with competitors for a set of prompts.

**Does ranking first in Google get me into ChatGPT?**
It helps but does not guarantee it. Strong Google rankings show relevance, but ChatGPT gives a lot of weight to agreement between outside sites and to how clearly it knows who you are. A site can hold position one and still be missing from the AI answer above it, if AI tools barely know the brand or cannot read its pages.

**Which matters more: my website or off-site mentions?**
Off-site mentions, usually. Your site lets AI tools read and quote you. Off-site mentions make them trust you. AI models give more weight to what independent sites say than to what you say about yourself, so consistent outside mentions do more than anything you write yourself.

**How is this different from SEO?**
It shares roughly 70% of the technical foundation with SEO: pages that crawlers can reach, rendering, structure, schema markup and freshness. It differs on the 30% that matters most for AI: agreement between outside sites and how clearly AI tools know who you are. See our full breakdown in [GEO vs SEO](/insights/geo-vs-seo/).

**How do I know if it is working?**
Run the same test prompts on a fixed routine and track how often your brand is named and your share of mentions over time. Automated trackers on their own disagree with each other. The reliable picture comes from careful weekly manual testing plus tracking of mentions.
